Migration from Fixed Transfer Car to an AMR Fleet

Key Integration Points and High‑Density WIP Layout Design for a Corrugated Packaging Plant
Recommended next step
Prepared for: Cascades — Engineering & Operations

Executive summary

This document summarises a migration concept that replaces a fixed transfer carriage and long conveyors with a coordinated AMR fleet and Trident handoff stations. The proposed architecture is designed to increase space efficiency, remove single points of failure, and deliver stable, JIT feeding to corrugator and converting lines.

Key expected outcomes: improved floor utilisation, reduced production starvation, lower mechanical OPEX and a phased migration path that minimises business interruption.

Key value drivers

Maximised floor space

Concentrated WIP blocks and flexible AMR lanes increase usable area and simplify reconfiguration.

Elimination of SPoF

Redundant AMRs plus Trident stations provide graceful degradation under failure.

Decoupled flows

Short local conveyors at Trident stations decouple transport from line insertion.

Reduced mechanical maintenance

Less continuous mechanical conveyance reduces wear and spare‑part complexity.

Material flow dynamics

1. Induction

Corrugator outputs are staged, identified and assigned for AMR pickup.

2. Deep‑lane WIP

AMRs move reels to dense storage islands reducing linear footprint.

3. JIT feeding

Trident stations deliver to lines when requested by MES/WCS.

4. Returns

Partial reels are routed back to WIP or warehouse via AMRs.

Engineering & integration considerations

  • WCS/Fleet Manager API for job dispatch, slot reservation and health monitoring.
  • Trident station IO handshake and safety interlocks.
  • Traffic orchestration for dual‑lane highway with lane discipline.
  • MES integration for job changes and preemptive refill logic.
  • Exception and fallback strategies for blocked stations and robot faults.
